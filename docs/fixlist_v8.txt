Forge v8 -- 15 October 2026
=============================

Audit of v7 (the "deep dive" pass)
----------------------------------
Every change in v7 was read line by line against v6 and re-tested here. They are
all real fixes and none of them is a regression:
* decode faults are now reported as SchemaErrors;
* `risk` is validated, which closes a release-gate bypass through spelling;
* the patch `add` at index 0;
* the interval zero bound and atan2's branch cut;
* the partial-revolve wedge, the chamfer and draft Lipschitz constants, and the
  sweep envelope;
* the compiler cache handing one feature another's geometry;
* watertight INCONCLUSIVE for slanted cylinders;
* measurement faults becoming BLOCKED evidence instead of a crash;
* budget, transport and Ollama fixes.
v7 is the base of v8.

What went wrong in the first runs with thinking (v6)
----------------------------------------------------
Thinking made the designs right more often. The L-bracket, the dome and the stepper
plate were all correct first time, with no repair rounds needed. It also made the
calls long, and Forge's transport could not cope:
* Every reply was read whole, with a 600 s timeout. The leg shell's designer was
  cut off at 600 s and sent again from the start. Its analyst was cut off twice,
  and so was its redesign. The run took 65 minutes and ended BLOCKED.
* Every role thought at the model's full effort. The side rail's requirements cost
  43 700 output tokens and 12 minutes, more than its design did.

Fixed
-----
* Streaming (forge_models/anthropic.py).
  - Replies are streamed. The timeout is now the longest silence allowed (5 min),
    not the longest answer, so a stall is caught in minutes.
  - An overload or a dropped connection mid-answer is retried.
  - A cut-off answer is read as a truncation, so the role asks again with more
    room.
  - The heartbeat line now says "thinking, about N tokens" or "writing, about N
    tokens".
  - A network that is briefly unreachable (the "getaddrinfo failed" run) is
    waited out over about 30 s instead of 7.
* Effort per role.
  - brief interpreter: low;
  - requirements engineer: medium;
  - designer and gate repairs: the model's full effort;
  - last look: medium;
  - clarifier: low.
  - --effort / -Effort overrides all of them. If the model refuses an effort
    level, Forge drops it for the rest of the run.
* Angles (forge_units, forge_expr, forge_ir).
  - A plain number in a slot declared in degrees is now that many degrees. The
    stepper plate's `360 / count` bolt-circle step had come out as 60 radians
    (3437.7 deg); the holes landed unevenly and an analyst round was spent on it.
  - A plain number added to an angle in degrees is also in degrees:
    `alpha + 10` with alpha = 30 deg is 40 deg, where it used to be 603.
  - Anything worked out with pi (or an inverse trigonometric function) stays in
    radians.
* Wall thickness (forge_geom/measure.py).
  - A ray counts as a wall only if it leaves through the face opposite the one it
    entered by (within 37 degrees).
  - The leg shell's top rim read 0.006 mm where a ray grazed out of a convex
    edge. A flared cup's rim read 0.03 mm. Both now read their real walls.
* The thinnest-wall claim (forge_roles/library.py).
  - "Minimum wall thickness = 8 mm" on a shell whose brief cuts holes and an
    opening through the wall was failing a correct part. The web between the
    opening and the holes the brief puts beside it is 3.75 mm.
  - The wall claim measures the walls, so the duplicate claim is now dropped, but
    only where the brief cuts through the walls.
* Material questions.
  - A record that ASKS what material the part is made of (the side rail's
    UNK_MATERIAL) no longer holds the part when nothing measured rests on the
    material (no mass, no margin). It stays in the report as an open question,
    as an assumed material already did.
  - "Continuous material around every hole" is no longer mistaken for a
    material.
* Analyst schema: `patches` now comes straight after `strategy`. Placed last,
  behind three optional fields, it was being left out, which cost a second call.
* run-design-claude.ps1: -MaxCost now defaults to 3.00. A run with a redesign can
  pass 2.00 with thinking on, and the ceiling is a hard stop.

Audit of v8 (16 October 2026)
-----------------------------
Every v8 change was diffed against v6 with the v7 edits subtracted, read, and
probed by running code. Found sound: the streamed transport (event
reassembly, the silence timeout, mid-answer errors, the out-of-credit case),
effort per role, the enum validation, the wall-thickness exit-face filter,
the material question eased only where nothing measured rests on it, the
hole that keeps its own position, and the analyst schema order. All 133
recorded real designer answers replay as before (132 parse, one refused for
a reason the model can act on); the fast modules pass (thinking runs 27,
regressions 38, units 58, expr 45, models 154, spec 231, patch 32).

The degree rule, probed on edge cases: `360 / count`, `alpha + 10`,
`180 - 2 * alpha`, `pi / 3`, `deg(atan2(y, x))`, `step * 2` and the radian
slots all come out right. Two forms do not, and both are the model's
spelling rather than the rule's: pi written as `3.14159` is now read as
degrees (1.05 deg for `2 * 3.14159 / 6`), and `atan2(y, x) * 180 / pi`
has always been 2578 deg, because the radian label survives the product.
The authoring guide said nothing about either; it now tells the designer
to write `pi` by name and `deg(...)` for an inverse trigonometric result.
(forge_ir/spec.py)

Noted and left: a dropped thinnest-wall claim is no longer reported as a
finding, but the measured thinnest wall is still written to the run's
measurements (`minimum_wall`), so the figure is not lost; and a material
question opened rather than asked means a run without --assume no longer
pauses on it, which is the intended trade when nothing measured reads the
material.

Addendum, after tier P (17 Oct)
-------------------------------
Thirteen of fourteen came out as expected. P11, the torus, was released wrong
with every claim passing, and the suite's own grading caught it. The cause was
Forge's, not the model's.
* The brief: "an 8 mm round section bent into a full ring 40 mm across at the
  centre line of the section". That is a ring 48 mm across.
  - Forge's size reading took the 40 as the outside and claimed 40 mm on X and
    on Y.
  - The first design was right (48 across) and failed both claims. The repairs
    saw that the claims contradicted the brief and gave up.
  - The redesign then satisfied every claim by building the ring on a 32 mm
    middle, under a parameter still called 40. It was released as PASS.
* Fixed (forge_roles/library.py):
  - A diameter measured to the middle is not taken as the part's outside:
    "at the centre line", "centreline diameter", "mean" or "pitch diameter",
    "inner diameter", "a 60 mm diameter bolt circle".
  - The ring reader takes such a figure as the ring's middle, so this torus is
    40 to the middle and 48 across.
  - Where the brief gives a ring's middle (or its inside), Forge claims the
    built ring's width on X and Y (here 48 mm) and its roundness. An extent
    claim set at the middle's figure is corrected to the outside. The released
    ring now fails those claims, and the first design passes.
  - The ring's volume bound uses the stated middle when there is one.
  - Across all 164 briefs Forge has on record, including the whole suite, the
    size reading changes for P11 and nothing else.

-------------------------------
Eleven of sixteen passed. All five failures were Forge's faults, and so was
one part that was released only after a wasteful repair (C06).
* C04 (stepped shaft) and C15 (dumbbell): blends the briefs never asked for,

  - Fixed: an assumption that the record says is the brief's own ("as

  - The record read "two ears ... 25 mm tall rising from its two ends" as the

Addendum, after tier L (18 Oct)
-------------------------------
Five of seven passed. L05 and L07 were Forge's faults.
* L07 (height limit): "The whole part must be no taller than 50 mm" was read
  as the part's height. The record's own "at most 50 mm" was turned into
  "exactly 50 mm", and a correct part (a 40 mm post on a 5 mm base, 45 mm
  tall) failed it. The repair then put in a 5 mm riser nobody asked for.
  - Fixed: a comparison is a limit however it is worded: "no taller than",
    "wider than", "fewer than", "must not exceed", "a height limit", "<=" or
    a "less than or equal" sign. "Other than" and "rather than" compare
    nothing. Across 703 briefs on record, this changes L07 and nothing else.
* L05 (mass limit that fails): a 20 mm cube of 1018 steel weighs 63 g
  solid, so a 50 g limit cannot be met. The suite expects Forge to say so.
  Instead, a repair kept the cube 20 mm on every side and shelled it inside
  to a 3 mm skin. The weight came in at 41 g and every claim passed. The
  last look saw the outside of a cube and released it. The part cannot be
  machined and looks solid in every view.
  - Fixed: a new measurement, sealed_voids, counts the pockets of air the
    part closes in on every side, on the surface it is already measured on.
    Where the record limits the part's weight or volume, and the brief has
    no word for air inside it (hollow, shell, sealed, walls, a cavity, a
    tank), the record also claims nothing is sealed inside. The shelled
    cube now fails that claim, so the repair is taken back and the weight
    fails honestly.
  - The failure analyst, the second design and the last look are told the
    same rule: change how the part the brief describes is built, never
    which part it is. Nothing the brief does not describe goes in or comes
    out to pass a check: no hollow, shell, pocket, riser or spacer. When the
    brief's own sizes and material cannot pass a limit, that is the answer:
    say which figures conflict, and by how much.
* The console: a Windows console reading a pipe showed every "+/-" in the
  evidence as a block. A console that is not UTF-8 is now written in plain
  ASCII: "+/-", ">=", "->", and "?" for anything with no plain spelling.
L02 (PLA cantilever) is right: it fails its safety factor honestly, at 0.73
against 2. It took 10 minutes, mostly one long analysis and a second design
that could not help.

Addendum, the hollow dome (19 Oct)
----------------------------------
"A PLA dome: exactly half of a 70 mm sphere, ... hollow on the inside" was
released with every check passing, and the slicer showed the dome closed
across its open side: a filled disc with a dark zigzag over it.
The part was right: a 2 mm shell, 14,549 mm^3, open on its flat side.
The STL was not. It drew a skin of no thickness across the opening, twice,
back to back: a solid half ball round a sealed half-ball void. The zigzag
was the slicer drawing two faces in one place. Forge's own measurements
said so (one piece of 89,778 mm^3 with a sealed void of 75,229 mm^3), and no
check read them.
Cause: the cavity was cut by the same plane that cuts the dome. Across the
opening the field is exactly nought and above it on either side. The mesh
grid's samples lay exactly on that plane, and a sample on the surface was
counted as material. That holds for any cut flush with a face whose plane
lands on the grid: a 50 x 50 x 25 tray with a flush pocket was drawn as a
closed box too. A part half as tall as it is wide puts its top there.
* Fixed: a sample within rounding of the surface is material only if there
  is material right beside it. It is read again a thousandth of a cell
  away, in 26 directions. A face, edge or corner of the part keeps it.
  A skin, line or point of no thickness does not. Two blocks touching face
  to face are still one solid. This is applied wherever the mesh code
  classifies a sample: the surface, the fine band, the floating-piece check
  and the meets-itself check.
* Guard: a mesh in which two sheets lie on each other (edges between
  different vertices at the same place) is not watertight. The extractor
  tries another grid, and GEOM_WATERTIGHT fails such a surface, saying it
  is a skin of no thickness. The check costs one sort when no two
  vertices coincide.
The dome now meshes as one piece of 14,523 mm^3 with no void. The tray is
open.

Addendum, the exo arm's upper rail (19 Oct)
-------------------------------------------
Part 2 of the exo-arm build: a 287 mm rail with four 8.2 mm bolt holes, two
22.1 mm bearing pockets and two 15 mm counterbores. All 32 claims passed,
measured on the design itself, and the design was right. The STL beside
the run was not: its 8.2 mm holes were 7.3 to 7.7 mm across at their
narrowest, its counterbores 13.5 mm and its bearing pockets 21.85 mm. The
part could not take its own bolts or bearings.
Cause: the STL was the surface the checks read, meshed 96 cells along the
part's longest side. That is 0.7 mm cells on a 70 mm part and 3 mm cells on
a 287 mm one. A hole is as round as its facets, and the flat facets cut
inside it. That is fine for counting holes and pieces, and wrong for a file
someone prints.
* Fixed: the STL is meshed again for printing, in 0.5 mm cells. Only the
  band of cells round the surface is sampled finely, so a large part costs
  its surface, not its volume. A part whose band will not fit the budget is
  meshed as finely as it fits, up to about 800,000 faces (some 40 MB). It
  must come out closed. A few edges shared by four faces are allowed only
  when each one is the grid's guess on a slanted surface, checked the way
  the watertight gate checks them. Otherwise the checks' surface is written,
  as before.
  On the rail: the holes are 8.13 to 8.17 mm, the pockets 22.03 mm and the
  counterbores 14.94 mm at their narrowest. 99% of the surface is within
  0.03 mm of the design; sharp edges are rounded by up to about 0.2 mm.
  This takes 6 s. The coil spring takes 27 s and the calf shell 37 s (its
  facets are 0.97 mm, 99% within 0.06 mm; the checks' surface was within
  1 mm).
  The listing says what was written, e.g. "0.44 mm facets for printing, 99%
  of the surface within 0.03 mm of the design". `forge export` meshes for
  printing too (--cell sets the facet size; --resolution gives the old
  grid). The STL is now written all at once, not one facet at a time: on
  the laptop's Linux VM, 364,000 facets one at a time took longer than
  three minutes. The bytes written are the same.
* The same rail had an 8 mm blend, which nobody asked for, where its round
  end meets the bar. Forge takes off roundings a brief doesn't ask for, but
  it read "round end", "fully rounded far end" and "inside radius" as words
  asking for rounding. Those words name a shape (a semicircular end, a
  bend), not a rounded edge, and no longer count. Across the 138 briefs on
  record (the suite, every run on the laptop, the exo-arm plan), this
  changes four, all of which name shapes: the two exo rails, the wrist link
  strip and P10's obround plate.

Addendum, the upper rail run again (19 Oct)
-------------------------------------------
The same brief, run again, was held after 27 minutes and $3.09. Every
claim on the rail passed except REQ_ROUND, which Forge added itself. This
time the record claimed the rail's 48 mm width at its round end as
bounding_box_y. Forge read "one end of the bar is a round end 48 mm in
diameter" as the part being 48 mm round, so it claimed X = 48 as well and

It was not caused by the STL fix. The rule that read the size has been
there since tier V; the first run's record just did not claim the 48.
* Fixed: a size said of a piece of the part is that piece's, not the
  part's. The nearest thing named before the figure in its clause decides:
  an end, a tab, a lug, a hole or a pocket always; a boss, post or hub only
  when it is said to stand on something and the brief doesn't call the part
  that. "It is a disc 80 mm across" and "a flange disc 50 mm across" are
  still the part.
  Across the 138 briefs on record this changes ten, all of them figures
  that belong to a piece: the two rails' round ends, pockets and boss,
  bosses and posts standing on plates (R13, C01, C11, the stepper plate),
  a 30.5 mm square of hole centres, a countersink, tabs' pivot holes on the
  calf shells, and the bracket's handle bar.
* The same run dropped an assumption that described the rail's far end as
  a fillet nobody asked for. That came from the morning's fix: the brief no
  longer "asked for rounding", so the assumption filter started working on

Addendum, the forearm rail's lug (19 Oct)
-----------------------------------------
Part 06 of the exo arm passed 34 of 34 and the lug was wrong. The brief:
"a lug 24 mm wide ... ends in a semicircle of radius 12 mm whose centre is
28 mm from the bar's centreline". The designer drew a 24 by 22 box, 16
thick, rounded `top_along_y` at 12. Those edges run along the lug and
round its section, which 16 mm of thickness limits to 8, so Forge clamped
it to 8 on the same edges. The lug came out square-ended seen from above,
its two top edges rounded, and the 20 mm boss on it overhung the rounding.
REQ_LUG_SEMICIRCLE_RADIUS read the parameter table (12) and passed. The
last look saw the fault, wrote its fix in words and no patch, and was
recorded "nothing to change".
* Fixed (kernel): a box can round only the two upright edges of one face:
  `edges: vertical_x_min / vertical_x_max / vertical_y_min /
  vertical_y_max`. A radius of half that face's width is a full round in
  plan; the rest of the box stays square, so a lug's root meets the bar
  along its whole width. The radius may reach the box's full length behind
  the face (12 on a 22 mm reach), which no four-cornered rounding can.
  The designer's description of `edges` now says top/bottom are z, and how
  a flat tab's end is rounded.
* Fixed (designer): a radius too big for the edges it was written on,
  that is a full round across one side of the plan, now goes on the
  upright edges of the free end. The free end is the one with a hole, boss
  or pin standing at the rounding's centre, or the one whose opposite face
  sits on another solid. With no such sign the old clamp stands.
  The rule that rounded both ends in plan ("both short ends fully
  rounded") still does, except where one end sits on another solid: that
  end is kept square, as rounding it full round would join it at a line.
* Fixed (designer): a `min(...)` that takes a requirement's parameter
  below its own figure by more than 0.05 mm sends the design back. The
  claim would pass on the parameter table against a part that does not
  have that figure.
* Fixed (last look): an answer with no patches is taken as "matches the
  brief" only when its strategy says nothing should change. A strategy
  that describes a fix is asked for its patches, like any repair. If it
  still writes none, the run says "the last look saw a fault and wrote no
  edit for it", with what it saw, not "nothing to change".

Addendum, the wrist link strip (19 Oct)
---------------------------------------
Part 11 passed 25 of 25 and printed square-ended. The brief: "a flat PLA
strip 110 mm long, 16 mm wide and 2 mm thick with both ends fully
rounded". The designer drew it correctly: a box rounded `width / 2` on its
upright edges. Forge took that rounding off as "a fillet nobody asked
for". Since the morning's fix, "fully rounded ends" counts as a shape and
not as a rounding, so the brief seemed to ask for no rounding at all.
That fix listed this strip among the four briefs it changed; what it did
to the strip's design was not checked. Nothing measured the ends, and the
last look, shown a picture with square ends, said they were round.
* Fixed: when a brief rounds its ends ("both ends fully rounded", "a
  fully rounded far end", "semicircular ends", "ends in a semicircle of
  radius 12 mm"), the roundings that draw an end are kept: a box's upright
  edges or the edges along it, and an outline's corners. Blends in
  junctions, fillets round a face and rounded extrusion ends are still
  taken off when nothing asks for them, so the upper rail's 8 mm blend
  stays off.
* Added: a new measurement, `ends_round:<r>`. It reads both ends of the
  part along its longer side, seen from above and through the middle of
  its thickness, and gives how far, in mm, they are from semicircles of
  radius r: the tip on the middle line, then the half-width at five places
  back from it, each side. A brief that rounds both ends fully, sizes the
  part by its width, isn't bent and has nothing sticking out gets
  REQ_ENDS_ROUND: at most 0.25 mm from semicircles of half the width. The
  strip as designed reads 0 mm; square-ended it reads 4.5 mm.
* Fixed: an interface on a flat sketch that one extrude is built from is
  read as that extrude's side. The strip's slot port named
  `slot_profile.region`, which measures nothing, and cost two repair
  rounds to be repointed. On record, the calf shell of 13 October had the
  same fault on its access opening's port; it now passes.
