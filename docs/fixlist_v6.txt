Forge v6 -- 14 October 2026
================================

From the six runs (L-bracket, leg shell, stepper plate, curved arm plate, dome, collar)
and the upper-arm side rail run.

Why Claude struggled
--------------------
* Every call was a FORCED tool call. On Claude Sonnet 5 a forced tool call is answered
  at once with no thinking at all, so each design was a whole CSG program written in a
  single pass with no room to work it out. Forge now lets a model that can think do so
  (adaptive thinking on Sonnet 5 and other new models, a token budget on older ones) and
  offers the tool instead of forcing it.
  - New flags: --thinking auto|adaptive|enabled|off and --effort low..max (also
    FORGE_THINKING / FORGE_EFFORT; -Thinking / -Effort in run-design-claude.ps1).
  - If the API refuses a thinking setting (an unbilled 400), Forge steps it down and
    remembers. An answer written in words is asked for in the tool, in the same
    conversation, with its thinking kept. A JSON answer written in the text is used.
    An answer cut off before it starts is treated as a truncation, so the role asks
    again with more room instead of resending the identical call.
  - Thinking calls get at least 600 s before they time out.

Forge's own faults (false PASS / needless rounds)
-------------------------------------------------
* L-bracket: the plate-layout repair took the longer side for the leg's length. It now
  reads the long way from the brief ("60 mm wide ... 50 mm long").
* Dome: halved, then shelled, so it came out sealed; and no claim checked that it was
  hollow. A shell that seals an opening the record claims is now moved before the cut.
  A wall assumption whose figure is only in its statement now yields the
  wall-thickness and opening claims.
* Stepper plate: slots the model had drawn were recut, measured from a blend-swollen
  envelope. Slots drawn as patterned boxes now count. A trim that clips a joined boss
  is settled. A chamfer drawn as an oversized chamfered box (whose chamfer missed the
  plate) is now built as the plate's own top-edge chamfer.
* Curved plate: held BLOCKED for a material assumption although nothing measured
  depends on a material. It is now kept as a guess in the report and does not hold the
  part. It still holds the part when mass or a safety margin is claimed.
* Analyst re-asks:
  - an abandon whose reason sits in its diagnosis is kept;
  - patches written inside the text are used;
  - refused answers are recorded in the run log;
  - a "placeholder" answer is sent back rather than applied.
* The analyst is now shown what every derived parameter actually comes to. On the leg
  shell it believed (z_bottom + z_top) / 2 was -37.5 when it was -12.5.

Upper-arm side rail
-------------------
* Two holes spaced 60 mm apart across a 30 mm rail (both designs did this) are turned
  to run along the rail. They are placed on solid plate with a wall all round, clear of
  the other holes, the cutouts and the boss.
* A 15 mm radius on the end edges of an 8 mm plate, which is half the plate's 30 mm
  width, now rounds the ends in plan (a stadium) instead of being clamped to 4 mm.
* Polygon profiles take corner_radius (new). "Rounded triangular cutouts" whose radius
  was put on the extrusion's end_radius now get it on the triangle's corners.
* A cutout the brief calls a cutout that was cut only part way through the plate (the
  sketch plane was at the plate's top face) now runs right through. This is not done
  when the brief speaks of pockets, recesses, depth and similar.

Kept as intended
----------------
* The side rail's "compliance standard" question holds the part for one explicit
  acceptance. It is a body-worn exoskeleton part, and Forge cannot check regulatory
  compliance. Use: forge accept <run>\document.forgeir.json
